2. How do you connect?

USB-C and Canon support are new and not tested on a real camera yet. If something fails, tap Send test report in the Report tab.

USB-C cable experimental

  1. On the a6100: MENU → Network → Ctrl w/ Smartphone → Off. Then MENU → Network → PC Remote Function → On, and USB Connection → PC Remote.
  2. On the 2000D: turn Wi-Fi off (Wi-Fi function → Disable) and set the mode dial to P, Tv, Av or M.
  3. Connect the camera to the phone with a data cable (camera Micro-USB to phone USB-C, or an OTG adapter) and switch the camera on.
  4. Tap Connect over USB-C and allow Framewise to use the camera. Framewise detects Sony or Canon itself and reconnects if you unplug and replug.

Canon 2000D · Wi-Fi experimental

  1. On the camera: MENU → Wi-Fi function (or Wireless communication settings → Wi-Fi function) → Remote control (EOS Utility).
  2. Choose Camera access point mode → Easy connection. The camera shows its network name (EOS2000D_…) and password.
  3. On the phone: Android Settings → Wi-Fi → join that network. Android may say “No internet”: choose to stay connected.
  4. Come back here and tap Connect to Canon. The first time, the camera may ask you to confirm the pairing with “Framewise”: choose OK. If it keeps waiting for EOS Utility, enter the camera IP below (often 192.168.1.2) and try again.

While on the camera’s Wi-Fi, AI and update checks may not work unless Android keeps mobile data on.

Sony a6100 · Wi-Fi

Framewise joins the a6100’s own Wi-Fi and shows its live view directly. You don’t need the Sony app, and AI keeps using your mobile data.

  1. On the a6100: MENU → Network → Ctrl w/ Smartphone → On. Then choose Connection.
  2. Press the camera’s Delete (trash) button to show the Wi-Fi name (DIRECT-…) and password.
  3. Type the password below and tap Connect to camera. Android asks to join the camera Wi-Fi: pick it and tap Connect.
  4. Close the Sony app first. The camera talks to one app at a time.

Camera Wi-Fi has no internet. AI and update checks use mobile data while you’re connected to the camera. Nothing is recorded: Framewise keeps only the newest frame in memory.

AI reads the scene using instructions tailored to this app and your a6100. The exposure engine uses your entered camera readings. No model has been trained on your photos.

Use a Gemini project on the Free tier for zero API charges. Google sets the quotas; billing-enabled projects can incur charges. This app never switches to a paid provider automatically.

Keys stay in app memory and are forgotten when the process closes. Frames are not stored by this app.

Local guidance works offline without any API key. Cloud scene reading sends the cropped preview to your selected service under its data terms. Google’s handling differs by region and project setup.

Test reports
Testing Framewise? A report helps fix problems without needing your phone. Tap Send test report and it goes straight to the Framewise developer.

What’s included: app version, phone model and Android version, camera model and connection type, the last 150 app events, errors and crashes, your shot history (settings and AI tips, no photos) and your Framewise settings. Never included: your AI API key, the camera Wi-Fi password, photos or live-view frames. Nothing is sent until you tap Send. Reports go automatically to the Framewise developer over a secure connection and are deleted after 90 days.
